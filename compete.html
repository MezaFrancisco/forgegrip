01 How It Works
Marshal Tap Timing

A Wave, Not a Station.

The usual mistake is scoping a tap screen to one station — but athletes spread out, so a marshal needs their own group, wherever each person actually is on the course. Each marshal gets a wave: every athlete's card shows their current segment and a live clock, and search-to-jump handles anyone out of sequence. Tapping "Next Seg" only ever advances that one athlete.

Every split is timed by the server, not the marshal's phone — so two devices with slightly different clocks can never disagree, and a retried tap can never double-count a result.

Shown with simulated event data.

Big-Screen Venue Display

Project It. Walk Away.

Multiple concurrent wave clocks, a 16-tick segment-progress bar per athlete (not a single blurry percentage), a "Finishing Soon" panel, and a live tap ticker across the bottom — reusing the same self-healing, auto-reconnecting wall-display technology already running your WOD board and leaderboard. Point a Fire TV Stick at it and it looks after itself.

A QR code on screen links straight to results — athletes claim their result without a staff member doing it for them.

Shown with simulated event data.

Per-Station Results

Every Station,
Not Just the Finish Time.

Every athlete gets an 8-axis radar on their own result — toggle between their percentile against everything the gym has ever logged on that station, their rank in that day's field, or how they did versus their own last race. Their weakest station is called out in amber: that's the actual coaching point, not just a finish time.

Every percentile carries its sample size, so a number built on four attempts can't pose as a real one — and a station a coach hasn't named yet shows up honestly as "not recognised" instead of a guess.

02 Where This Goes

Every HYROX table already carries the same two keys everything else in ForgeGrip joins through — gym and member. The moment a second gym comes on board, station-by-station comparison across gyms is a normal join, not a rebuild.
